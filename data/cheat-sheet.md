# CBT - Cheat Sheet


## The Cognitive Model

```
Situation → Automatic Thought → Emotion → Physical Sensation → Behavior
                    ↑                                              |
                    └──────────── reinforces ───────────────────────┘
```

It's not the situation that causes the emotion.
It's the **interpretation** you make of it.


## Automatic Thoughts

Spontaneous, rapid, and involuntary thoughts that arise in response to a
situation. Usually negative and accepted as truth without questioning.

**How to identify:**
- They arise suddenly, without effort
- They feel like absolute truth at the time
- They generate intense emotion
- They are short and specific ("nobody likes me", "it's my fault")


## Core Beliefs

Deep convictions about yourself, others, and the world. Formed in
childhood and reinforced throughout life. They are the root of automatic thoughts.

| Category       | Examples                                             |
|----------------|------------------------------------------------------|
| Helplessness   | "I am incapable", "I am weak", "I am incompetent"   |
| Unlovability   | "I am undesirable", "Nobody loves me", "I am rejected"|
| Worthlessness  | "I have no value", "I am a failure", "I am useless" |


## 13 Cognitive Distortions

| #  | Distortion                   | What it is                                           | Example                                          |
|----|------------------------------|------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------------------------|
| 1  | Mind reading                 | Assuming you know what others think                  | "He looked at me because he suspects me"         |
| 2  | Personalization              | Attributing to yourself the cause of external events | "It's my fault"                                  |
| 3  | Catastrophizing              | Imagining the worst possible scenario                | "Everything will go wrong and there's no going back"|
| 4  | Selective abstraction        | Focusing on the negative and ignoring the rest       | "Everyone praised it but one criticized, it was bad"|
| 5  | Arbitrary inference          | Concluding without evidence                          | "Nobody called, they don't like me"              |
| 6  | Overgeneralization           | Broad conclusion from an isolated event              | "I failed once, I always fail"                   |
| 7  | Magnification/minimization   | Exaggerating the negative, diminishing the positive  | "My mistake was huge, my success was luck"        |
| 8  | All-or-nothing thinking      | Absolute terms: always, never, everyone              | "If it's not perfect, it's a failure"            |
| 9  | Fortune telling              | Predicting things will go wrong without basis        | "I know the interview will be a disaster"        |
| 10 | Emotional reasoning          | Believing it's true because you feel it is           | "I feel like a failure, so I am one"             |
| 11 | Labeling                     | Fixed labels for yourself or others                  | "I'm useless" / "He's bad"                      |
| 12 | Should statements            | How things must be vs how they are                   | "I should be stronger"                           |
| 13 | Disqualifying the positive   | Ignoring positive experiences                        | "I passed, but it was luck"                      |


## Cognitive Restructuring (4 steps)

```
1. Identify     → What is the automatic thought?
2. Examine      → What evidence is for and against?
3. Restructure  → What is a more balanced thought?
4. Evaluate     → How do I feel now with this new perspective?
```


## Questions to Challenge Thoughts

- "What real evidence do I have for this?"
- "Am I confusing a thought with a fact?"
- "What would I tell a friend in this situation?"
- "What's the worst that could happen? And the most likely?"
- "Will this still matter in a year?"
- "Am I assuming what the other person thinks without asking?"
- "Have I been through something similar and it turned out fine?"
- "Am I using always/never/everyone/no one?"


## Safety Behavior

Actions you take to avoid anxiety but that **reinforce** the fear:

- Avoiding places, people, or times
- Leaving situations quickly
- Avoiding eye contact
- Walking on tiptoes to not be noticed
- Checking your phone to avoid interacting
- Asking someone else to go in your place

**The problem:** immediate relief → reinforces the belief that there was real danger
**The goal:** reduce gradually, not all at once


## Exposure Hierarchy (6 steps)

To face fears gradually:

```
1. List feared situations from least to most difficult
2. Identify the safety behaviors in each one
3. Face the situation WITHOUT the safety behavior
4. Stay in the situation until anxiety drops by half on its own
5. Repeat until the fear diminishes
6. Record your progress
```


## Emotions and Physical Sensations

| Emotion      | Common physical sensations                  |
|--------------|---------------------------------------------|
| Anxiety      | Racing heart, sweating, trembling           |
| Fear         | Chest tightness, shortness of breath, chills|
| Anger        | Muscle tension, facial flushing, heat       |
| Sadness      | Fatigue, heaviness in the body, pain        |
| Shame        | Facial flushing, heat, urge to disappear    |
| Guilt        | Nausea, stomach pain                        |


## Habits That Support Mental Health

- **Nutrition:** prefer real food, cook
- **Exercise:** regular physical activity, generates endorphins
- **Sleep:** consistent times for sleeping and waking
- **Balance:** leisure and rest alongside work
- **Interests:** hobbies that absorb your attention
- **Environment:** keep your space organized and pleasant
- **Relationships:** spend time with people regularly
- **Self-acceptance:** don't demand perfection from yourself
- **Goals:** have goals aligned with your values
- **Humor:** don't take yourself too seriously


## Fighting Depression (4 strategies)

1. **Self-compassion** - treat yourself as you would treat a friend
2. **Reduce rumination** - focus on the present and the external, don't get stuck in your thoughts
3. **Reframe thoughts** - recognize that negative thoughts are symptoms, not reality
4. **Schedule activities** - gradually reintroduce activities that bring pleasure or a sense of accomplishment


## Tips for Daily Recording

- Record **on the same day** (memory distorts quickly)
- It doesn't need to be perfect, what matters is doing it
- The emotion **doesn't need to drop to zero** - reducing it is already progress
- Patterns emerge with **consistency** - record whenever you can
- Re-reading old records helps you see your evolution
